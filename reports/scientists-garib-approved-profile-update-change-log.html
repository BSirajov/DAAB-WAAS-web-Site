Garib Murshudov, PhD

Group Leader, Computational Structural Biology, MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, Cambridge, United Kingdom; Head of the Laboratory of Computational Structural Biology, Institute of Molecular Biology, Ministry of Science and Education of the Republic of Azerbaijan, Baku, Azerbaijan

Garib Murshudov specializes in computational structural biology, developing mathematical, statistical, and computational methods for studying the three-dimensional structures of biological macromolecules. His work combines experimental data from crystallography and cryo-electron microscopy with prior structural and chemical knowledge to produce reliable atomic models. His group is based at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology in Cambridge.

He studied applied mathematics at Baku State University from 1980 to 1985 and earned his PhD at the Institute of Crystallography in Moscow. His doctoral research investigated the three-dimensional structure of catalase from the bacterium Micrococcus lysodeikticus.

He worked at the Institute of Physics in Baku from 1985 to 1987 and at the Institute of Crystallography in Moscow from 1987 to 1993. From 1993 to 2011, he held research positions at the University of York, including a Wellcome Trust Senior Research Fellowship and a Wellcome Trust University Research Fellowship. He has led a research group at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology since 2011.

He has contributed to the development of software for determining, refining, and validating macromolecular structures, including REFMAC, BALBES, Servalcat, AceDRG, and EMDA. These tools support the analysis of experimental data, the refinement of atomic models, and the incorporation of chemical and structural information into molecular modeling.

His publications include research articles in Nature, Science, PNAS, and Acta Crystallographica, as well as book chapters on crystallography and structural refinement. He has taught statistics, computational chemistry, and computational structural biology, and has organized international scientific workshops in countries including Brazil, Uruguay, the United States, Japan, China, and Thailand.

He holds the honorary title of Honored Scientist of the Republic of Azerbaijan and is a member of the European Molecular Biology Organization (EMBO) and the Royal Statistical Society. He has also served on the CCP4 executive committee.

Research interests:

  • Macromolecular crystallography and the structural analysis of proteins and nucleic acids.
  • Cryo-electron microscopy and the interpretation of experimental structural data.
  • Maximum likelihood and Bayesian statistical methods in structural biology.
  • Software development for molecular modeling, atomic model refinement, and structure validation.
